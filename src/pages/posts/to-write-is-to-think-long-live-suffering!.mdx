With the advent of the revolution of LLMs, I am finding it harder and harder to suppress the luddite in me. I say this because I have tried to offload my reasoning and cognitive abilities to these probabilistic neural networks, and for the most part they are fun to use in activities or knowledge that I do not care about. And I am glad that there is now technology that frees the mind of things we dislike or even feel friction about; this prompted me to think about what do I now *like* doing? or even -- what do I want to feel friction with? what is worth thinking about?


I have to say that even though my career as a manual coder was short-lived, I loved every second of it. What pulled me into this world of software was specifically the friction one felt tackling a problem, coming up with a *horrible* solution (a solution nonetheless) and iteratively refine said solution to something more elegant and meaningful. That is gone! at least in the economical sense, writing code by hand will now be a hobby that one has to actively pursue, and I think I will continue to do so in my free time.

Contrasting that with writing, I am afraid here is where my luddite dispositions might prevail. *"Writing is thinking. To write well is to think clearly. That's why it's so hard."*,  this was said by David McCullough, and I could not agree the more. As I am even writing this mess of a journal or blog or article -- I do not even know what to classify it as. I can feel the gears in my brain turning and churning thoughts onto this digital landscape of a webpage. This is the true essence of writing, it allows one's thoughts to flourish, to be polished. But most importantly, it gives way to express the inner self, the soul of the writer. This is something a stochastic machine cannot replicate; it can "imitate" for sure, but as one reads on, you are bound to find cracks in the armor. 


And so my friends this is a pledge, a pledge to think. Because writing and thinking are twins made from the same cloth, this I am not willing to give up, I am not willing to give up the true essence and shape of my soul. As opposite as it may feel, this topic ties back to human suffering, what it is and how it shapes us. 

*“The discipline of suffering, of great suffering … has been the sole cause of every enhancement in humanity so far.” -- Friedrich Nietzsche* 

As I recount most of human technological advances, it always leads back to the question of suffering. In fact the entire reason for having the privileges we have as modern day humans is because of the suffering that was present in the lives of our forefathers. I might even push this further to say that even the advent of us having modern computing is a result of WW2, though this is not a radical idea or thought, I'm always surprised that people are surprised when they find out.  Maybe because of the comforts afforded to us in our age, we sometimes find it difficult to remember the origins of our ingenuity.

This makes me wonder, what kind of suffering awaits us as humans, with the rapid advancements of LLMs? what kind of friction will we have to overcome? this I do not know. 

But what we can do, is to remain in control of our thinking, of our emotions, of our souls! these fatal elements are what make us human. So write my friends, think my friends and look forward to suffering, whether great or small, for in that we will find the way.
